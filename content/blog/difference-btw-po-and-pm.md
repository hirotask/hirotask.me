## TL;DR (For Busy Readers)

* The biggest difference between a Product Owner (PO) and a Project Manager (PM) lies in their mission. **The PO’s mission is “doing the right thing” (building the right product), while the PM’s mission is “doing the thing right” (building the product correctly).**
* The PO focuses on **value**, while the PM focuses on **planning**. The PO handles **Why/What**, while the PM handles **How/When**. Understanding this distinction is the first step to avoiding the “PO becoming a PM” problem.

---

## Responsibilities of the Product Owner

A Product Owner is generally responsible for the following:

* **Defining and communicating the product vision:** Establish the direction and goals of the product and share them with all stakeholders.

* **Managing the product backlog:** Create and maintain a list of features and requirements (the product backlog), prioritizing them based on business value and customer needs.

* **Stakeholder alignment:** Gather and reflect the needs of various stakeholders—customers, executives, and users—into the product.
*(Unlike PMs, POs are not responsible for constraints such as scope, time, cost, and quality.)*

* **Maximizing value:** Make decisions to ensure that the value of what the development team delivers is maximized relative to investment.

Success as a Product Owner means releasing a product that is accepted by the market and delivers high value. Therefore, the primary focus is always on the value the product provides. Their thinking revolves around questions like:
“What should we really build?” and “How can we increase the product’s value?”

### How POs Engage with Stakeholders in Meetings

* **Primary stakeholders:** Customers, product users, market analysts, company executives, sales and marketing teams

* **Approach:**
  * **Listening (Input):** Thoroughly understand the background and needs behind “why this is needed.” Collect requests about “what features are desired” and understand expectations for the product.
  * **Communicating (Output):** Explain the product vision and feature priorities derived from gathered insights to executives and related departments. Clearly and passionately convey “why we are building this” to gain alignment and support.

### How POs Work with Development Members

* **Relationship:** Act as a **member of the development team**, working from the same perspective. To developers, they often appear as a reliable senior figure—someone who represents the team’s concerns externally.

* **Approach:**
  * **Explaining requirements and answering questions:** In sprint planning and similar meetings, explain the purpose, background, and specifications of upcoming features (user stories). By clearly communicating the **Why** (e.g., “We want to solve this user problem”), they boost team motivation. They remain available to answer questions during development.
  * **Acceptance and feedback:** Verify that completed features meet requirements (acceptance testing) and provide feedback—always from the perspective of whether it delivers value to the user.

## Responsibilities of the Project Manager

A Project Manager is generally responsible for the following:

* **Project planning:** Define scope, schedule, cost, and quality plans.

* **Resource management:** Secure and allocate necessary resources such as personnel, budget, and equipment.

* **Progress, issue, and risk management:** Monitor whether the project is on track, address issues when they arise, and manage potential risks.

* **Task management and reporting:** Manage team tasks and report project status to executives and stakeholders.

Success as a Project Manager means executing the project according to plan. Therefore, emphasis is placed on accurate estimation and task management. Their thinking revolves around:
“Are we on schedule?” and “Are there any issues or risks? If so, how do we resolve them?”

### How PMs Engage with Stakeholders in Meetings

In meetings, the PM speaks as the **person accountable for the project**, focusing on How and When.

* **Primary stakeholders:** Clients, external partners, internal departments (legal, accounting, PR, etc.)

* **Approach:**
  * **Reporting (Output):** Regularly report progress, including “when” the project will be completed and whether it is within budget. When issues or risks arise, present their impact and proposed solutions to support decision-making.

  * **Coordination (Input/Output):** Coordinate “how” the project will proceed with relevant parties. For example, negotiating contracts with external vendors or aligning delivery schedules.

### How PMs Work with Development Members

A Project Manager acts as a **shield**, protecting the team from unnecessary external demands and pressure while ensuring a productive environment.

* **Relationship:** Operates as a **manager/supporter** of the development team, maintaining a step-back perspective to oversee the whole. Developers often see them like a construction site supervisor.

* **Approach:**

  * **Task management and progress tracking:** Monitor each member’s task progress and ensure there are no delays. If delays occur, identify causes and work together on solutions.

  * **Removing obstacles (blockers):** Eliminate any barriers that prevent developers from focusing on their work. Whether it’s insufficient hardware or lack of cooperation from other departments, they actively resolve such issues. They also protect the team from unnecessary external pressure.
